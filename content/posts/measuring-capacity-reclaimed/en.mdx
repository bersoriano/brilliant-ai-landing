Most automation business cases multiply a guessed hourly rate by a guessed time saving and call the result savings. A finance team sees through that in a minute, and it should. If an agent or a workflow is worth running, the evidence is in your own week: how much work arrived, how much of it the workflow prepared, and how long that work used to take.

This is how we measure it.

## Start with a baseline week

Pick one task: invoices, referrals, client follow-ups. For one normal week, note how many items arrived and roughly how long each took. Ranges are fine. “Ten to fifteen minutes an invoice” is more honest than “12.4 minutes”, and it’s what the person doing the work can actually tell you.

Write down what made the slow ones slow. That list is the scope.

## Count three things every week

- **Items in.** Everything that arrived.
- **Items prepared.** What the workflow took from arrival to a card a person can decide on.
- **Items sent to a person.** The exceptions.

The third number isn’t a failure rate. It’s the part of the work that needs judgment. It should go down only because the rules got clearer, never because the hard cases are being routed somewhere else.

## Convert to hours, not dollars

Hours reclaimed are the items prepared, times the baseline time per item, minus the time your team still spends reviewing them. Report it as a range. Hours are something a team lead can check against their own week. A dollar figure starts an argument about the hourly rate instead of a conversation about the work.

## The money is your number

The value of reclaimed capacity isn’t a cash saving unless a cost actually goes away. If you want a figure, use your own hourly value, and say what the hours went to: the close finished a day earlier, referrals were scheduled sooner, the backlog of client documents cleared. That sentence is worth more than the number.

## What not to report

- Percentages with decimals.
- A “touchless rate” on its own. It rises when the hard cases go elsewhere.
- Savings that assume the hours disappear.

## A monthly view that holds up

- Items in, prepared, and sent to a person
- Hours reclaimed, as a range
- Time from arrival to decision
- Exceptions by reason, most common first
