**Who this is for:** controllers, finance managers and owners who run an ERP, or are choosing one, and want to know what AI inside it can do today.

<Takeaway>
AI in the ERP works best around the core of the system: answering questions in plain language, preparing matches and entries for review, and flagging anomalies early. The ERP stays the controlled core, and the results depend on clean master data and on approvals that stay with people.
</Takeaway>

For decades, ERP (enterprise resource planning) systems have been the backbone few people enjoy using: expensive to implement, hard to navigate, and mostly useful for recording what already happened. Finance teams reconciled. Procurement chased approvals. Operations waited on reports. The software captured transactions; people did the thinking and the heavy lifting.

That’s starting to change. Generative AI, and especially agentic AI, is turning the ERP into something that helps do the work, not just record it.[^softices] Early adopters report EBIT improvements of 5% or more, and McKinsey estimates that AI agents can cut ERP implementation effort and program duration by at least half.[^mckinsey]

Here’s what that looks like in practice for companies that already run SAP, Oracle Fusion, Microsoft Dynamics 365 or similar platforms:

| Where AI helps | What it does | What stays with your team |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Questions about your data | Answers in plain language from live data, with links to the source records | Deciding what the answer means |
| Accounts payable | Captures invoices, matches them to POs and receipts, codes them, routes the exceptions | Approving exceptions, posting, paying |
| Reconciliation | Matches bank transactions and prepares journal entries | Reviewing and posting the entries |
| Forecasts and anomalies | Updates forecasts more often; flags duplicate payments and unusual expenses as they happen | Acting on the flag |

## 1. Ask your ERP questions in plain language

The most mature use, and the one people find useful right away, is asking the ERP questions in plain language. Instead of clicking through nested screens or waiting for a report, a finance leader can ask: “What were our top five suppliers by spend last quarter, and which ones are trending above budget?” The answer comes back structured, drawn from live data, with links back to the source records.[^nasscom]

Microsoft, SAP (through Joule) and Oracle have all shipped this.[^vendors] If you’ve spent years clicking through ERP menus, that’s a real change: you skip the menus instead of learning them.

## 2. AI agents for accounts payable and reconciliation

Generative AI drafts and explains. Agentic AI does the work.[^technologyadvice]

- **Accounts payable:** agents capture invoices from several channels, match them to purchase orders and receipts, code them, and route only the true exceptions.
- **Reconciliation:** agents match bank transactions and prepare journal entries for review.[^versich-agents]
- **Procurement:** agents read supplier communications, flag orders at risk, and show the knock-on effect on sales and inventory.
- **Manufacturing and supply chain:** agents monitor equipment sensor data for predictive maintenance and continuously adjust safety stock based on demand signals, weather and port congestion.[^manufacturingdive]

Gartner projected that about 40% of enterprise applications would include task-specific AI agents by the end of 2026, up from under 5% the year before.[^gartner] In ERP, vendors now ship agents that run under the system’s existing security roles and audit trails.[^vendors]

One thing to keep in mind with AP: the hours are in the exceptions, not the clean matches. See [Why invoice automation stalls on exceptions, and how to fix it](/blog/why-invoice-automation-stalls).

## 3. From month-end reports to continuous forecasts and exception management

Traditional ERPs are good at telling you what happened. With AI, they can also show what is likely to happen next and what to do about it.

- **Forecasts:** demand forecasts move from static monthly plans to weekly (or faster) updates that take in outside signals.[^erpsoftwareblog]
- **Cash flow:** models take in payment behavior and different scenarios.
- **Anomalies:** duplicate payments and unusual expenses get flagged the day they happen, not at month-end.[^synlabs] In one example McKinsey describes, AI invoice analysis found more than $10 million in value leakage in four weeks.[^mckinsey-leakage]

What usually works: the ERP stays the controlled core (rules, controls and master data), and agents work around it. They read context, prepare actions, and hand off to a person when judgment or approval is needed.

## The real constraint isn’t the AI model. It’s your data and your process.

Data quality, consistent processes and clear controls still decide the outcome.[^versich-ready] If you lay AI over messy ERP data, you get messy results. The companies seeing a measurable effect on the P&L tend to do the unglamorous work first: clean master data, consistent processes, and agents with clear limits and a full audit trail.

McKinsey’s broader research shows that only a minority of companies are getting meaningful company-wide EBIT lift from AI so far, and the gap between high performers and everyone else is widening.[^state-of-ai] In ERP, that usually means redesigning the workflow instead of automating the old one as it is, and tracking the impact instead of celebrating a pilot demo.

## Where to start with AI in your ERP

1. **Start with high-volume, well-bounded processes** where the pain is clear: invoice processing, reconciliation, demand forecasting or collections prioritization.[^versich-ready]
2. **Ask for agents that work under your ERP’s existing security** and keep a full audit trail.
3. **Clean the master data first.** Vendor, customer and item records come before any agent, not in parallel with it.
4. **Keep approvals with named people.** Agents prepare entries; a person posts and pays. [Approval workflow design that doesn’t create a new bottleneck](/blog/approval-points-without-bottlenecks) covers how to keep that fast.
5. **Measure time saved, exception rates, forecast accuracy and cash-cycle impact**, not just “AI adoption.” [Measuring automation ROI](/blog/measuring-capacity-reclaimed) shows how to report the hours honestly.

None of this means replacing your ERP. The point is to get more out of the one you already have.

## Next step: list the three things your team fixes by hand

Ask the people who work in the ERP every day which three things they most often fix by hand: a mismatched invoice, an unreconciled bank line, a duplicate vendor. Those are the first candidates for an agent to prepare, and the first rules to write down.
